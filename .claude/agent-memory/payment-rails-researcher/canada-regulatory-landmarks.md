# Canada regulatory landmarks (reference)

- **Gambling is a provincial monopoly to authorize** (Criminal Code s. 207 "conduct and manage"). A private operator cannot self-license; it goes *through* a province or *under* a registered operator. **Bill C-218 (2021)** legalized single-event sports betting, still routed through provincial authority.
- **Esports outcome betting is already regulated betting in Ontario** — so MoneyMatch's products are "event betting," not a skill-contest carve-out (you're a book).
- **Ontario (iGaming Ontario / AGCO):** live since Apr 2022; ~48 operators / 82 sites; March 2026 handle CAD $9.59B; esports-inclusive. Direct operator: **~$100k/yr per site + ~20% revenue share** + full compliance. Solo route = **B2B supplier or operator partnership**, not direct.
- **Alberta (AGLC / AiGC):** launches **2026-07-13**; **$50k application + $150k/yr** (pricier than Ontario) but a new market with fewer incumbents.
- **Other provinces:** Crown-corp monopolies (BCLC, Loto-Québec, ALC…) — private operators generally can't be independently licensed.
- **Kahnawake (KGC):** offshore-style licence; bans US traffic; no provincial legitimacy or banking acceptance. Avoid as a foundation.
- **FINTRAC:** MSB registration **mandatory** for money transmission / currency exchange / **dealing in virtual currency**; unregistered = criminal (up to CAD $2M / 5 yrs). **Travel Rule ≥ CAD $1,000; LVTR ≥ CAD $10,000.** Crypto guarantees the MSB trigger.
- **Tax:** recreational player winnings are **NOT taxable** in Canada (CRA "windfall") — a UX advantage over US W-2G/1099. Operator owes corporate tax + (if licensed) provincial revenue share.
